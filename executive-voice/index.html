Day 1 · Mechanical Motor

Mon / Thu — Pacing & Articulation

Decouple racing thoughts from the mouth. Build jaw release, vowel openness, and a calm 120–130 WPM cadence you can hold under pressure.

Session length
~20–25 min
Target: 120–130 WPM Vowel openness Jaw release Order: Breath → Metronome (8–10m) → Pen (8–10m)

For beginners · start here

What should I do today? (Mon / Thu)

Today is only about speaking slowly and clearly. Do not worry about fancy words or meeting frameworks. Your only job: stop rushing.

Do these 3 things in order

  1. Sit quietly and breathe for 60 seconds (belly rises, chest stays calm).
  2. Open a metronome → set 120 → read any page aloud with the click for 2 minutes.
  3. Put a clean pen behind your front side teeth → read aloud 2 minutes → remove pen → read the same lines again.

Simple example (metronome)

Click… “The” · click… “pro” · click… “ject” · click… “is” · click… “de” · click… “layed”.

One syllable per click. If you race ahead of the click, stop and restart.

Simple example (pen drill)

With the pen in: read “We need better monitoring.” Slowly force every word to be understandable. After removing the pen, the same sentence should feel lighter and clearer.

Done for today when: you finished both drills once, and you plan one meeting answer where you pause 1 second before speaking.

Exercise 1 of 2 · ~8–10 min

120 WPM Metronome Pacing

Angle: pacing & motor control — decouples high-speed thoughts from physical speech execution. Force the mouth to wait for the click.

Motor Control

Technical protocol

  1. Open a free metronome app (or Google “Metronome”). Set to 120 BPM.
  2. Pick any book or article. Start reading aloud.
  3. Speak exactly one syllable or word per beat. Do not rush ahead of the click.
  4. Vowel openness: drop the jaw slightly on every vowel (ah / eh / ee / oh / oo). Do not chew words with a tight mouth.
  5. If you surge ahead, stop, reset to the beat, and continue. Force the mouth to wait.
  6. Hold a steady, calm rhythm for 2 full minutes without breaking cadence. Repeat once if the first attempt breaks.

Tool

Free Metronome App · Google Metronome

KPI / Success metric

Steady rhythm for 2 minutes without breaking cadence

Everyday transfer

In standups, consciously slow speech ~20%. Let a full second of silence pass before answering any question.

Exercise 2 of 2 · ~8–10 min

The Horizontal Propeller (Pen Drill)

Angle: muscular over-articulation — releases jaw tightness and forces dynamic tongue movement so mumbling has nowhere to hide.

Articulation

Technical protocol

  1. Place a clean pen/pencil horizontally behind your canine teeth.
  2. Read a complex article aloud for 2 minutes, forcing maximum intelligibility despite the obstruction.
  3. Remove the pen. Immediately re-read the same text without it.
  4. Notice the contrast: lighter jaw, more resonance, crisper consonants.

Tool

Pen/pencil + Harvard Business Review (or any dense article)

KPI / Success metric

Instant vocal resonance, lighter jaw tension, immediate speech crispness after removal

Form cue

Do not bite hard. Keep the pen stable with canines only. Over-enunciate vowels — open the mouth more than feels natural.

Session close-out checklist

  • ✓ Metronome held 2 min at 120 BPM without surge
  • ✓ Pen drill completed; post-pen read felt crisp
  • ✓ Note one standup/meeting today where you will insert a 1-second pause before answering

Day 2 · Fuel Tank

Tue / Fri — Lexicon & Phonics

Build lightning-fast pathways from tech jargon to executive business value. Fix lazy pronunciation so AI (and humans) hear you clearly the first time.

Session length
~20–25 min
Recall < 1.5s Syllabic stress Dictation > 95% Order: Breath → Value sprints (10–12m) → Phonetics (8–10m)

For beginners · start here

What should I do today? (Tue / Fri)

Today is about finding the right words fast and saying them clearly. Think of it as vocabulary practice + pronunciation practice.

Do these 3 things in order

  1. Breathe for 60 seconds to settle.
  2. Pick one tech phrase → start a 45-second timer → speak business benefits out loud (not technical details).
  3. Pick 3 hard words → check pronunciation on Cambridge → speak each into phone voice-to-text until it spells them correctly.

Simple example (value sprint)

Tech phrase: “System downtime”

Say in 45 seconds: “This protects revenue, reduces customer trust damage, avoids SLA penalties, improves business continuity, and lowers support cost.”

You are listing business impact words — not explaining servers.

Simple example (phonetics)

Word: infrastructure. Look up IPA on Cambridge. Speak it into Notes dictation. If it writes “infra structure” or something else wrong, adjust and retry until it writes the correct word.

Done for today when: you completed at least 3 value sprints + 3 words that voice-to-text got right on the first try.

Exercise 1 of 2 · ~10–12 min

Tech-to-Value Sprints

Angle: active vocabulary recall — builds fast neural pathways so you never go blank mid-sentence.

Vocabulary

Technical protocol

  1. Pick a common IT/tech phrase (e.g. database migration, system downtime, API latency).
  2. Start a 45-second countdown on your phone.
  3. Latency rule: after you name the tech phrase, the first value term must start within 1.5 seconds.
  4. State the corporate business-value impact out loud using high-status terms (see bank below).
  5. Do not pause more than 2 seconds between terms. Keep retrieving.
  6. Repeat with a new tech word. Aim for 3–4 sprints per session.

Tool

Smartphone countdown timer

5+ high-tier management terms in 45 seconds; pauses ≤ 2 seconds

The Phonetic Breakdown Check

Phonics

Technical protocol

  2. Look up each word’s IPA phonetic spelling on Cambridge Dictionary Online.
  3. Speak each word into your phone’s voice-to-text engine.
  4. If the AI misspells it, adjust tongue/lip position and stress. Retry until correct.
  5. Log words that failed first attempt — revisit them next Tue/Fri.

Day 3 · The Engine

Wed / Sat — Spontaneous Synthesizer

Install mental scaffolds (PREP / SCQA) so you never run out of structure. Replace filler panic with controlled silence.

Session length
~25–30 min
Filler words = 0 PREP / SCQA 60s no dead-ends Order: Breath → Structure ×3 (12–15m) → Filler valve (5–8m)

For beginners · start here

What should I do today? (Wed / Sat)

Today is about speaking in a clear structure and removing “um / uh / like.” You practice answering like a manager: Point → Reason → Example → Point.

Do these 3 things in order

  1. Breathe for 60 seconds.
  2. Pick a topic → speak for 60 seconds using PREP (or SCQA). Do this 3 times.
  3. Record yourself talking about your day for 2 minutes. Whenever you feel an “um” coming, close your lips, stay silent, then continue.

Simple PREP example (topic: “Should we hire?”)

P: Yes, we should hire one backend engineer. R: Current load is delaying customer features. E: Last sprint, 2 of 5 stories slipped due to capacity. P: So I recommend opening the requisition this week.

Simple filler example

Instead of: “So um… we should uh… fix this…” → pause silently → then say: “We should fix this before Friday.”

Done for today when: you did 3 structured 60-second talks + one 2-minute recording with as few fillers as possible (target zero).

Exercise 1 of 2 · ~12–15 min

The Executive Structure Drill

Angle: on-the-fly sentence building — provides instant mental structures so you never run out of things to say.

Frameworks

PREP

  • Point — state your position in one sentence
  • Reason — why it matters (business lens)
  • Example — one concrete case or metric
  • Point — restated close / ask

Spoken example (topic: observability)

P: We should invest in observability this quarter.

R: Because outage diagnosis currently burns engineering hours and customer trust.

E: Last month’s incident took 90 minutes to isolate; with tracing it would have been under 15.

P: So I recommend a 6-week observability sprint before the next release freeze.

SCQA

  • Situation — shared context
  • Complication — what changed / risk
  • Question — the decision needed
  • Answer — your recommendation

Spoken example (topic: tech debt)

S: We ship features weekly on the current platform.

C: Defect escape rate has doubled, and release risk is rising.

Q: Do we keep feature velocity, or pause for a stability sprint?

A: Pause two sprints for stability — it protects revenue and restores delivery predictability.

Technical protocol

  1. Generate a random topic (Random Word Generator or pick from the bank below).
  2. Speak for 60 seconds using PREP or SCQA — choose before you start.
  3. Do not restart mid-stream. If you blank, use silence, then resume the next structural slot.
  4. Record optional audio so you can audit logical breaks later.
  5. Do 3 rounds: 2 PREP + 1 SCQA (or reverse).

Tool

Random Word Generator + optional voice recorder

KPI / Success metric

Zero logical breaks or dead-ends for 60 seconds straight

60-second topic bank

• Should we freeze feature work for a stability sprint?
• Why observability investment pays for itself
• Migrating a monolith vs. strangler pattern
• How to explain an outage to non-technical leadership
• Prioritizing security debt vs. new revenue features
• Making the case for a platform team

Exercise 2 of 2 · ~5–8 min

The Silent Exhaust Valve

Angle: eradicate vocal fillers — replaces frantic filler sounds (um, uh, like) with powerful silence.

Fillers

Technical protocol

  1. Open the built-in phone voice recorder.
  2. Record a 2-minute audio note detailing your day (or a work update).
  3. The moment an “um” or “ah” approaches because your mind is thinking: freeze, close your lips completely, and swallow.
  4. Resume only when the next word is ready. Silence is the exhaust valve — not filler sound.
  5. Replay and count fillers. Log the number. Target: zero.

Tool

Built-in phone voice recorder

KPI / Success metric

Zero filler words per 2-minute recording

Month-2 field target

On live calls: fillers under 3 per 10-minute speech block

Session close-out checklist

  • ✓ 3 × 60s deliveries completed (mix of PREP + SCQA) with no dead-ends
  • ✓ Filler recording counted; target 0 (or note the count and retry once)
  • ✓ In one meeting today, answer using PREP or SCQA out loud without notes

Active Functional Recovery

Sunday — Breath & Nervous System

Calm the system that causes rapid-fire adrenaline speech. No performance drills — only respiration control and diaphragmatic anchoring.

Session length
~15–20 min

For beginners · start here

What should I do today? (Sunday)

Sunday is a rest-and-calm day for speaking. No hard drills. You only train calm breathing so you do not rush when nervous in meetings.

Do these 3 things in order

  1. Sit or lie down. One hand on belly, one on chest.
  2. Do 8–10 slow breaths: inhale 4 counts (belly up) → hold 2 → exhale 6.
  3. Ask yourself one mock meeting question, take one calm breath, then answer in one short sentence.

Simple breathing example

Inhale: “1-2-3-4” (belly rises) → Hold: “1-2” → Exhale: “1-2-3-4-5-6” (slow). Repeat.

Simple mock-question example

Question: “Are we ready for Friday’s release?”

You: (one breath) “Not yet — we need one more test cycle, then we can confirm.”

Done for today when: you finished 8–10 calm breaths + one mock answer after a single breath. Then stop — no metronome, no pen drill today.

Diaphragmatic Anchoring Protocol

  1. Sit upright or lie on your back. One hand on chest, one on belly.
  2. Inhale through the nose for 4 counts — belly rises, chest stays quiet.
  3. Hold gently for 2 counts.
  4. Exhale through the mouth for 6 counts, as if fogging a glass slowly.
  5. Repeat for 8–10 cycles. Then speak one calm sentence on the exhale only (e.g. “I will answer after I breathe.”).

Focus metrics

Respiration control · Diaphragmatic anchoring

Success signal

Heart rate feels slower; speech urge no longer feels rushed

Meeting transfer drill

Before any high-stakes answer this week: take one diaphragmatic breath, then speak. Practice this once on Sunday as a rehearsal with a mock question.

Session close-out checklist

  • ✓ 8–10 diaphragmatic cycles completed without chest-dominant breathing
  • ✓ One mock high-stakes question answered after a single breath
  • ✓ Light week review: which KPI failed most often Mon–Sat? Plan one fix

Live transfer playbook

Meetings — what to say in the real world

Lab drills only work if you reuse them in standups, status calls, and architecture reviews. Copy these patterns. Keep them short.

For beginners · start here

How to use this tab

Before a meeting, pick one script below. In the meeting: breathe once → use the script → stop. Do not explain every technical detail unless asked.

Daily transfer rule: every practice day must include one real meeting use of pause / value language / PREP / silence.

1) Daily standup update

Pattern: Done → Doing → Blocker → Ask

Example:

“Yesterday I finished the auth fix. Today I’m validating edge cases. One blocker: waiting on staging access. Ask: can we unblock that by noon?”

Transfer skill: 1-second pause before you start.

2) Status to a manager (non-technical)

Pattern: Business impact → Current state → Next action

Instead of: “The queue is lagging and the consumer offset is behind…”

Say: “This delay risks customer-facing timeouts. We’re at 70% recovery. Next action: finish the patch by 4 PM and retest.”

Transfer skill: Tech → Value language.

3) Architecture / design review (SCQA)

Pattern: Situation → Complication → Question → Answer

S: We currently deploy as one service.

C: Release risk and rollback time are rising.

Q: Do we keep one deploy unit or split the critical path?

A: Split the payment path this quarter to protect revenue continuity.

Transfer skill: SCQA without notes.

4) Recommendation ask (PREP)

Pattern: Point → Reason → Example → Point

P: We should freeze new features for one sprint.

R: Defect escape is hurting customer trust.

E: Two severity-1 issues landed in the last release.

P: I recommend a stability sprint starting Monday.

Transfer skill: end with a clear ask.

5) When you don’t know yet

High-status alternative to rambling

Instead of: “Um… maybe… I think… not sure…”

Say: “I don’t have a confirmed answer yet. I’ll check X and come back by 3 PM with a recommendation.”

Transfer skill: silence + commitment, not filler.

6) Incident / outage update

Pattern: Impact → What we know → What we’re doing → ETA

Example:

“Customer checkout is degraded. Root cause is still under investigation. We’re applying a temporary rate limit now. Next update in 15 minutes.”

Transfer skill: calm pace under adrenaline.

Meeting-day mini checklist

  • ✓ Choose one pattern before the call
  • ✓ Take one belly breath before your first sentence
  • ✓ Prefer silence over “um”
  • ✓ Close with a recommendation or a timed follow-up

Periodized Progression — 90-Day Block

Three monthly phases. The Week 1→4 themes below are the skill ladder you deepen each month — do not skip Month 1 mechanics to chase “executive presence.”

How to read this: Keep running the same weekly split (Mon–Sun) for all 90 days. What changes is emphasis — Month 1 prioritizes Day 1 drills, Month 2 prioritizes Day 2, Month 3 prioritizes Day 3 + live leadership moments.

Week 1

Mechanical Calibration

  • • Pace 120–130 WPM
  • • Articulation crispness

Week 2

Neural Retrieval

  • • Recall < 1.5s
  • • Dictation accuracy > 95%

Week 3

Structural Flow

  • • Flawless PREP alignment
  • • Fillers reduced > 80%

Week 4

Executive Delivery

  • • Public-speaking ready
  • • Balanced, poised cadence

Month 1 · Days 1–30 — Mechanical Calibration

Focus entirely on mechanical pacing and pen drills. Intentionally drag speech rate down to 120 WPM. In everyday team standups, consciously slow speech by ~20%. Let a full second of silence pass before you answer any question.

Exit criteria: can hold 120 BPM for 2 minutes · post-pen speech feels crisp without thinking · 1s pause habit started in meetings

Month 2 · Days 31–60 — Neural Speed-Up & Vocabulary Injection

Transition focus to vocabulary sprints. Build lightning-fast pathways to retrieve executive terms instead of technical descriptions. Record calls and ensure filler words drop under 3 per 10-minute speech block.

Exit criteria: 5+ value terms / 45s · first-try dictation on daily word trio · fillers < 3 / 10 min on real calls

Month 3 · Days 61–90 — Executive Presence & Public Mastery

Master PREP and SCQA. Every day, pick a random tech concept and map it into an oral PREP delivery within 60 seconds with zero preparation. Volunteer to lead high-visibility cross-functional syncs or architecture reviews.

Exit criteria: cold PREP in 60s · zero fillers in 2-min lab recording · led at least one visible sync using SCQA

Daily Month-3 add-on (even on Mon/Tue/Sun): cold PREP — random tech concept → 60s oral delivery → no notes, no restart.

Quick troubleshooting

Still rushing? Stay on Month-1 emphasis another week. Metronome before every meeting day.
Going blank? Drill Tech-to-Value with the executive bank visible, then hide it and retry.
Fillers persist? Do Silent Exhaust Valve daily for 5 days; count fillers out loud after each replay.
Adrenaline speech? Double Sunday breath work; take one breath before every answer for a full week.

Missed-day protocol

How to measure WPM simply

  1. Copy a 120-word paragraph into Notes.
  2. Start a 60-second timer and read aloud at your practice pace.
  3. Finish exactly at 60s ≈ 120 WPM. Finish early = still too fast.
  4. Target band for this lab: finish around 55–60 seconds for 120 words (≈120–130 WPM).

North-star outcome
